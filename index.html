San Diego County

Built around San Diego
homes and power bills.

In San Diego, when you use electricity can matter almost as much as how much you use. SDG&E’s most expensive hours are typically late afternoon into evening; solar production peaks closer to midday. “Find My System” below uses your ZIP, your bill and San Diego’s own rate structure — not a national average.

  • SDG&E delivers power countywide; San Diego, Chula Vista, La Mesa, Encinitas, National City and Imperial Beach also get generation through San Diego Community Power.
  • Coastal vs. inland sun exposure changes what a system can realistically produce.
  • Renters, condos and townhomes are common across the county — sizing here often starts smaller than a typical rooftop quote.

Storage — optional

What battery
adds.

Solar works on its own. A battery adds three things.

  • Use now — solar offsets what your home is using while it’s being produced.
  • Save for later — stored energy can be used after the sun goes down.
  • Backup — the battery’s own outlets can power select devices during an outage. Solar itself shuts off during an outage, for safety.

Questions

What to know before you order.

Can I start with a smaller system?

Yes. The smallest system is 400 W: two panels and one microinverter.

Can I add more later?

Yes. Systems grow in 400 W steps. Storage grows by adding units, up to 4 kWh per set. Expansion is limited by your circuit and by local rules - confirm before assuming unlimited growth.

Do I need a battery?

No. Solar works on its own. A battery lets you use more of what you make, after the sun goes down, and adds limited backup for select devices during an outage.

Where can the panels go?

Balconies, patios, yards and railings. The panels are semi-flexible and sit on stands rather than being bolted to a roof.

How does it connect to my home?

Panels connect to a microinverter, which connects to a dedicated outdoor outlet. A power meter watches what your home is using so the system can match its output. Some setups need a Smart Breaker instead of a dedicated outlet - we'll help confirm which applies to you.

What happens during a power outage?

Grid-connected solar shuts off during an outage, for safety. If you have battery storage, its own backup outlets can keep select directly-connected devices running - this is not whole-home backup.

Is this legal to install without a permit in California?

Not guaranteed yet. A bill that would streamline small plug-in solar systems (SB 868) has passed the State Senate and is moving through the Assembly, but it is not law as of this writing. Permit, HOA and utility requirements currently still depend on your address - confirm locally before ordering.

Can I move the system?

The panels sit on stands and the battery plugs into an outlet, so components are not permanently fixed to the building. Moving a system is a question for your installer and your local rules.

What rules or approvals may apply?

Requirements depend on your address: your local electrical and building rules, your utility, and your building, landlord or HOA policy. Confirm these locally, and use a licensed electrician where required, before ordering.
