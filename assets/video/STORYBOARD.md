### ⚠ Rail-mounted configuration — two open problems

**1. It reverses decision 2 and is not supported by the source.** The brochure shows balcony
rail hardware only with **framed glass** panels (p.4-p.5, and those renders carry the
third-party `emperysolar` watermark). The **semi-flexible** panels are shown only **on
stands** (p.7). Rail-mounting the semi-flexible panel is therefore a configuration the
supplied material does not document. The owner has approved it for the film; it must not
become a product claim on the retail page until the manufacturer confirms in writing that a
balcony rail mount exists for this panel.

**2. No attachment method is visible, and none may be invented.** In K3 the panels read as
adhering to the railing face with no bracket, clamp or rail. That holds at K3's distance. It
will not hold as the camera closes in: K4 and especially K5 put the attachment line near the
subject, where "how is this held on?" becomes unavoidable. Inventing hardware is forbidden by
the owner's own constraint and by this document.

**Mitigation adopted for K4:** frame obliquely **along** the panel run so the top edge and
thin profile carry the shot, keeping the attachment line low, soft or out of frame.

**⚠ Outcome at K4: small mounting clips appeared anyway.** Fine metallic tabs are visible
where the panel bottoms meet the wooden rail cap, and a darker bracket where the leftmost
panel meets the post. They are small and unresolved at K4's distance, and the owner approved
the frame. **They are invented hardware and correspond to nothing in the supplied
references.** They must never be shown resolved, described in copy, or presented as a
product part.

**Mitigation adopted for K5:** frame the macro **mid-span, between mounting points**, on the
top edge and laminate face only. No mount enters the frame, so nothing invented is resolved.

**⚠ Product fidelity drift.** The panels as rendered read as thin *rigid framed modules*
rather than the *semi-flexible* laminate in the Craftstrom references. K5 recovered the
material character well at macro distance, but the wider frames (K3, K4) still read as rigid
modules. This is a known drift from source and should be stated as such to the manufacturer
before any of these frames are used as product marketing.

**⚠ Verification required** before any balcony-mount depiction is published: confirmed
existence and specification of a rail mount for the semi-flexible panel, plus wind, ballast
and anchoring guidance. None is present in the supplied material.

## 6. Continuity method

The journey only reads as one unbroken fall if both ends of every segment are fixed before
the video is made. That is why the 11 keyframes are generated and approved first: they are
the segment boundaries.

**If the web app offers a last-frame / end-frame / keyframe control** (to be confirmed —
§1), each segment is made by supplying its two neighbouring keyframes:

```
S4   first frame = K3      last frame = K4
S5   first frame = K4      last frame = K5
```

Segment 4 is required to end on exactly the image segment 5 begins on — the same file,
used twice. The seam is a frame match by construction, with no extraction step.

**If the web app offers only a starting image**, chain forward instead: generate S1 from
K0, take its true final frame, and use that as S2's starting image. Continuity still holds
at each join, but the journey drifts from the approved keyframes as error accumulates, so
each segment is approved before the next is started. This path needs frame extraction —
`ffmpeg` is not installed on this machine and would need to be, or the final frame can be
exported from Runway's own editor if it offers that.

Which of these applies is the single most important thing the account inspection settles.

**Joining.** Segments are concatenated in order. Sharing a boundary keyframe means the seam
needs no dissolve; a 2–3 frame cross-dissolve is applied only if a residual pop survives
review.

## 7. Playback: size, memory, compatibility

You asked not to adopt canvas frame-sequence playback until the numbers were on the table.
Here they are, and **the recommendation is against it.**

### Option A — canvas frame sequence (not recommended)

50 s at 12 fps = **600 frames**; at 15 fps = 750.

| Encoding | Per frame | 600 frames |
|---|---|---|
| 1280×720 WebP q75 | ~45–70 KB | **27–42 MB** |
| 1600×900 WebP q75 | ~70–110 KB | **42–66 MB** |

**Memory is the disqualifier.** A decoded 1280×720 RGBA frame is 3.7 MB. Holding all 600
decoded would be ~2.2 GB — impossible. It would need a rolling decode window of ~60 frames
(~220 MB resident) with constant `createImageBitmap` churn, which is heavy on mobile and
fragile when scrolling fast. Combined with a 27–42 MB download, this is the wrong trade.

### Option B — ten short scrub-optimized MP4s (recommended)

One file per segment, matching the ten scroll bands exactly.

| | |
|---|---|
| Encoding | H.264 High, 1280×720, 24 fps, CRF 23, **GOP 6** (dense keyframes for seeking) |
| Per segment | 5 s ≈ **2.0–3.0 MB** |
| Full journey | **20–30 MB**, but only ~2.5 MB before the hero is usable |
| Resident memory | 2 decoders (current + next), ~30–60 MB |
| Loading | Segment 1 eager; each subsequent segment prefetched one band ahead |

Seeking inside a 5 s clip with a 6-frame GOP is fast and smooth everywhere. Splitting by
segment also means a failed or re-generated segment replaces one small file rather than
forcing a re-encode of a 50 s master.

**Browser compatibility.** H.264 in MP4 is universally supported. `currentTime` scrubbing on
short dense-keyframe clips is reliable in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari. iOS Safari needs
`muted` and `playsinline` (both required anyway) and does not like many simultaneous video
elements — capping at two loaded decoders is deliberate. An AV1/WebM alternate can be added
later behind `<source>` negotiation for ~30% smaller files.

**Not adopted:** a single joined 50 s MP4. At a scrub-friendly GOP it lands around 25–50 MB
as one blocking download, with no progressive entry and a full re-encode on any change.

### What stays unchanged

The scroll driver is playback-agnostic. It already publishes progress as `--p` (0→1); that
becomes a segment index plus an in-segment `currentTime`. Reverse scrolling works because
playback is a pure function of scroll position with no animation state to unwind. Sticky
pinning, the track, caption stepping, the `(min-width: 900px) and (min-height: 600px)` gate,
the reduced-motion gate, the static fallback, the retail transition and every lower section
are untouched.
