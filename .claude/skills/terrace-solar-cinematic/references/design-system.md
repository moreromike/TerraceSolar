# Terrace Solar design system

## Premise

Design around “energy that fits the space.” The panel should feel integrated with the architecture rather than imposed on it.

## Visual language

- Warm morning or late-afternoon residential light.
- Deep mineral blue or charcoal, warm off-white, and one restrained solar-orange accent.
- Thin lines and layered planes that echo the panel profile.
- Architectural crops, natural materials, believable terraces, and generous negative space.
- Product imagery remains crisp and factual. Decorative treatments never obscure cells, frame, connections, or mounts.

Avoid pure black and pure white canvases. Use the accent for the primary CTA, focus states, and one or two meaningful details only.

## Type

Choose a characterful but highly legible display face, a quiet body face, and optionally a restrained mono for measurements. Load only the weights used. Do not default to Inter or Roboto as the display face.

## Signature interaction

Use one meaningful interaction, not a collection of tricks. Good options:

- A thin-profile comparison controlled by scroll or drag.
- A modular 400 W configurator that adds panels without inventing price or output beyond listed sizes.
- A system-flow diagram that reveals panel, inverter, power meter, home, and battery in order.

The interaction must have a clear keyboard path and an immediate reduced-motion state.

## Page rhythm

Do not repeat the same card grid in adjacent sections. Alternate editorial layouts: full-bleed product moment, measured specification strip, diagram, split narrative, and FAQ. The page should feel directed, not templated.

## Copy tone

Calm, direct, and specific. Prefer “Designed around terraces and balconies” over inflated sustainability language. Do not use vague claims such as revolutionary, game-changing, best-in-class, effortless, or future-proof.
